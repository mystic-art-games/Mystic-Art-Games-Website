About Helianthe

Helianthe is a solarpunk, asymmetric, competitive card game for two players about rebuilding a shared world.

Each player becomes a Patron with a different vision for Aurelia's future. Instead of defeating an opponent in combat, players shape the shared board. The first to fulfill their Patron's vision wins.

A world built together in different ways

Helianthe takes place in Aurelia, a land recovering after the Great Eclipse. Players share the same geography but pursue different visions of renewal.

The board begins almost empty. As the players build Terrain and place Entities, its ecosystems, communities, and energy networks tell the story of the match.

Factions and visions

Aurelia's factions offer four paths forward: Verdant restores ecosystems, Brightworks builds responsibly powered infrastructure, Flowbound connects communities, and Dreamwardens preserve memory.

At a glance

  • One fixed board with 19 Nodes.
  • Four Terrain types: Symbiosis, Flow, Verdant, and Radiance.
  • Two card types: Entities, which stay on Terrain cards, and one-time Events.
  • Players take alternating turns until a Patron fulfills their individual victory goal.

Rules

Learn to play, then use the sections below as a reference during a match.

Start here

Helianthe is a two-player game on one shared board of 19 Nodes. Each player has a Patron with an individual victory goal and a 30-card deck containing 16 Entities and 14 Events.

  1. Place the 19-Node board, Terrain cards, Crisis and protection markers, Lumens, and each player's Patron.
  2. During Founding, one player creates Terrain on the central Node; the other creates Terrain on an adjacent Node. Each places their starting Entity on matching Terrain. The second player takes the first turn.
  3. Shuffle the remaining cards into your Horizon and draw five. If a starting hand has fewer than two Entities, shuffle and draw again.

Alternating turns

Players take turns until a Patron fulfills their individual victory goal. On your turn:

  1. Reset both Patrons' once-per-turn abilities and draw one card from your Horizon.
  2. Evoke as many Events from hand as you wish, resolving each and putting it in its owner's Archive. You may also take one Entity Play action or one Tend action. Events can be played before or after that action.
  3. Resolve triggered abilities and check both Patron goals after each Event or action. A fulfilled goal ends the game immediately.
  4. End effects lasting until the end of the turn, then pass play to your opponent.

Play

Your one Entity action is either to Evoke an Entity from hand, resolve its When Evoked effect, then Place it on matching Terrain and resolve When Placed; or Shape with an Entity from hand to create matching Terrain on a legal blank Node and resolve only When Shaped, if present. A Shaped Entity goes to its owner's Archive.

Tend

Instead of your Entity action, choose one: Free a Node containing your Entity from Crisis, draw one card from your Horizon, or pass.

Board and cards

A Node is one complete hex, with its Terrain, Entity, Condition, and markers. A shaped Node has one Terrain card and may have at most one Entity. Its Condition is either Crisis or none.

Terrain

  • Symbiosis: living ecosystems and mutual dependence.
  • Flow: water, movement, and retention.
  • Verdant: community, memory, and renewal.
  • Radiance: technology, energy, and production.

Card types

Entity cards remain on matching Terrain and may represent places, characters, objects, or institutions. Event cards resolve once and go to their owner's Archive. An Event's visual affiliation does not give it a Terrain value or change its effect.

Create Terrain first: a blank Node cannot hold an Entity. The first Terrain is created on the central Node. Every later Terrain creation must use a blank Node adjacent to at least one shaped Node. This rule applies to Events and Shape.

To Shape, put an Entity from hand face up in its owner's Archive, create its matching Terrain on a legal blank Node, then resolve its When Shaped effect, if any. Its When Evoked, When Placed, and local effects do not resolve.

Events can always be Evoked. Choose any required objects while resolving the effect, perform as much as possible, then put the Event in its owner's Archive. Cards have no individual Play Conditions.

Mechanics and markers

Crisis

Affect a Node with Crisis to give it the Crisis Condition. Free a Node from Crisis to remove that Condition. Affecting a Node already in Crisis leaves it in Crisis. Some Patron goals require Nodes without Crisis.

Protect / Unprotect

Protect a Node to protect both its Terrain and Entity. The next attempt to Dissolve either one or Affect the Node with Crisis is prevented; Unprotect it instead. An effect may also Unprotect directly. A Node cannot be Protected more than once. Protection does not prevent Transform or changes to Lumens.

Dissolve / Transform

Dissolve an Entity to move it to its owner's Archive while its Terrain remains. Dissolve Terrain to make its Node blank, moving any Entity there to its owner's Archive and removing Crisis, protection, and Lumens. Transform replaces Terrain on a shaped Node; its Entity and Condition remain unless an effect says otherwise. If Radiance becomes another Terrain, remove its Lumens.

Lumens

Lumens are markers on Radiance Nodes, not a separate currency. Each Radiance Node can hold at most two. A Patron's victory goal may count Lumens across the board.

Victory

Each Patron has a different vision of Aurelia's future, expressed as a goal on the shared board. Check both goals after each fully resolved action. The first player to fulfill their Patron's goal wins immediately. If both goals become fulfilled after the same action, the players share victory.

See for individual Patron goals and abilities.

Glossary and card keywords

Horizon
Your face-down draw pile.
Archive
Your face-up pile for discarded cards, resolved Events, Dissolved Entities, and Entities used to Shape Terrain.
Recall
Move an Entity or Event from its owner's Archive to the destination stated by an effect. Recall alone does not Evoke, Place, or Shape.
Create Terrain
Place a Terrain card on a legal blank Node. This differs from Transforming existing Terrain.
Evoke
Invoke an Entity or Event from hand. Resolve an Entity's When Evoked effect before Placing it; resolve an Event and then Archive it.
Place
Put an Evoked Entity on matching Terrain, then resolve its When Placed effect.
Shape
Archive an Entity from hand as a Play action to create its matching Terrain on a legal blank Node. Resolve only its When Shaped effect, if any.
Node
One complete hex of the Realm, including its Terrain, Entity, Condition, and markers.
Transform
Replace a shaped Node's Terrain, preserving its Entity and Condition unless an effect says otherwise.

Factions and Patrons

Factions express a way of rebuilding the world. Patrons bring those visions into a match through their abilities and victory goals. Faction identity does not restrict which Terrain cards a deck may contain.

Symbiosis

Verdant

Civilization should support more life than it consumes. Verdant favors connected ecosystems, diversity, rewilding, and renewal.

Myrr, the Rootweaver

Myrr's goal is a connected group of five Symbiosis Nodes, each with an Entity and without Crisis. Once per turn, after Symbiosis is shaped or it is Freed from Crisis, Myrr draws a card and discards a card.

Radiance

Brightworks

The future must be consciously built and responsibly powered. Brightworks focuses on energy, infrastructure, efficiency, and managed strain.

Solen, Architect of Dawn

Solen's goal is eight Lumens across Radiance Nodes. Once per turn, after Radiance is shaped or it is Freed from Crisis, Solen may add a Lumen to that Node if it has room, or draw a card.

World concept

Flowbound

No community can survive alone. Flowbound emphasizes water, movement, shared spaces, and chains of adjacent Nodes.

A playable Patron goal has not been defined yet.

World concept

Dreamwardens

A future without memory repeats old catastrophes. Dreamwardens explore preservation, recovery, and interaction with the Archive.
