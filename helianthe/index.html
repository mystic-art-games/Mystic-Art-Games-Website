About Helianthe

Helianthe is a solarpunk, asymmetric, competitive card game for two players about rebuilding a shared world.

Aurelia is a world that survived the Great Eclipse. Each player chooses a Patron with a different vision for the future. The first player to achieve their Patron's goal wins.

A world built together in different ways

Each match takes place in one recovering region of Aurelia. Players shape its ecology with Terrain cards; Entities establish places, people, organisms, objects, and institutions; Events make one-time changes. The Patrons seek lasting renewal but disagree about what it should become. They compete for the same limited space, even when both benefit from a change.

The first Terrain card can be placed in any of the 20 spaces on the 4 × 5 board. Each later card joins the growing layout by a side or a corner; Entities can then occupy matching Terrain.

Factions and visions

Aurelia's factions offer four paths forward: Verdant restores ecosystems, Brightworks builds responsibly powered infrastructure, Flowbound connects communities, and Dreamwardens preserve connections.

At a glance

  • One growing layout of rectangular Terrain cards, with a separate Terrain supply for each player.
  • Four Terrain types: Symbiosis, Flow, Verdant, and Radiance.
  • Three card types: Terrain builds the shared layout, Entities stay on Terrain, and Events resolve once.
  • Players take alternating turns until a Patron fulfills their individual victory goal.

Rules

Learn to play, then use the sections below as a reference during a match.

Start here

Helianthe is a two-player card game with one shared, growing Terrain layout. Choose a Patron and shape the World to fulfill their vision. In card text, the World means the Terrain cards on the table and Entities placed on them: the part of Aurelia represented in this match. Both Archives are outside it. Blank positions are existing land whose ecological identity has not yet been shaped. Each player has a Patron, a deck of at least 30 Entity and Event cards in any proportion, and a separate supply of Terrain cards.

  1. Place your Patron in your area, shuffle your Entity and Event deck into your Horizon, and draw five cards. The World starts without Terrain or Entities.
  2. Both players roll a die. The player with the higher roll chooses who takes the first turn; reroll ties.
  3. The starting player may take one mulligan: choose any number of cards from your hand, put them on the bottom of your Horizon, then draw the same number. Do not shuffle.
  4. After the starting player finishes or declines their mulligan, the second player may take one mulligan in the same way.
  5. The starting player begins the first turn. After Beginning of Turn, they skip Draw and proceed directly to Main Phase.

Four phases of a turn

Players take alternating turns until a Patron fulfills their individual victory goal. Each turn has four phases:

  1. Beginning of Turn: Resolve beginning-of-turn effects, including Verdant's Fertilize tokens, and reset once-per-turn Patron abilities. Resolve reactions and check Patron goals after each effect.
  2. Draw: Draw one card from your Horizon. The starting player skips this phase on their first turn and proceeds directly from Beginning of Turn to Main Phase.
  3. Main Phase: Evoke any number of Events from hand, resolving and archiving each one. You may take up to one Entity Play action: Place an Entity on matching Terrain already in the World, or Shape with an Entity to create matching Terrain on a legal blank Node. Events may be played before or after that action. If you play no Events and take no Entity Play action this turn, you may draw one additional card from your Horizon, then end your Main Phase. Shape counts as an Entity Play action. Resolve reactions and check both Patron goals after each Event or action; a fulfilled goal ends the game immediately.
  4. End of Turn: End effects lasting until the end of the turn, then pass play to your opponent.

Place an Entity

Evoke an Entity from hand, resolve its When Evoked effect, then Place it on an existing Node with matching Terrain and resolve When Placed.

Shape with an Entity

Put an Entity from hand face up in its owner's Archive and create its matching Terrain on a legal blank Node. Resolve only its When Shaped effect, if present. The Entity does not enter the World.

Terrain and cards

A Node is one Terrain card in the shared layout, with any Entity, Condition, Lumens, and markers on it. It may have at most one Entity. Its Condition is either Crisis or none. A Node can have at most one Crisis or one protection marker, never both. The board has 4 rows and 5 columns: 20 spaces for Terrain cards. A printed board is optional.

Terrain

  • Symbiosis: living ecosystems and mutual dependence.
  • Flow: water, movement, and retention.
  • Verdant: community, memory, and renewal.
  • Radiance: technology, energy, and production.

Card types

Terrain cards build the shared layout from each player's separate supply. Entity cards remain on matching Terrain and may represent places, characters, objects, or institutions. Event cards resolve once and go to their owner's Archive. An Event's visual affiliation does not give it a Terrain value or change its effect.

Create Terrain first: a blank Node cannot hold an Entity. Place the first Terrain card in any of the 20 spaces of the 4 × 5 board. Each later card must fill a free space inside that board directly beside an existing Terrain card, including one of the four diagonals. Cards touching by side or corner are adjacent. Take the card from your separate Terrain supply. If all Terrain is removed, the next card may again start in any space. This rule applies to Events and Shape.

To Shape, put an Entity from hand face up in its owner's Archive, create its matching Terrain on a legal blank Node, then resolve its When Shaped effect, if any. Its When Evoked, When Placed, and local effects do not resolve.

During the Main Phase, Events can always be Evoked. Choose any required objects while resolving the effect, perform as much as possible, then put the Event in its owner's Archive. Cards have no individual Play Conditions.

Mechanics and markers

Crisis

Affect a Node with Crisis to give it one Crisis marker. Affecting a Node already in Crisis changes nothing. If it is Protected, remove its protection instead; the Node ends with neither marker. Free a Node from Crisis to remove that Condition. Some Patron goals require Nodes without Crisis.

Protect / Unprotect

Protect a Node to protect both its Terrain and Entity. A Node can have only one protection marker; another Protect changes nothing. If it has Crisis, Protect removes Crisis instead of adding protection, leaving neither marker. The next attempt to Dissolve its Terrain or Entity is prevented; Unprotect it instead. An effect may also Unprotect directly. Protection does not prevent Transform or changes to Lumens. Moving Crisis or protection removes it from the source and applies it to the destination under these same rules.

Dissolve / Transform

Dissolve an Entity to move it to its owner's Archive while its Terrain remains. Dissolve Terrain to return its card to its owner's Terrain supply, move any Entity there to its owner's Archive, and remove Crisis, protection, and Lumens. Transform returns the old Terrain card to its owner's supply and replaces it in the same position with a card from the acting player's supply; its Entity and Condition remain unless an effect says otherwise. Destroying or Transforming Terrain removes all its Lumens and Entanglements. Destroying an Entity alone leaves those Terrain markers and links in place.

Lumens

Lumens are a spendable resource on Radiance Terrain. A Node normally holds up to 5. Going above 5 causes Overload: Affect it with Crisis, then immediately spend excess Lumens through legal effects or lose them. Only Lumens on Radiance Terrain without Crisis count toward the 15-Lumen vision. See Glossary for the full rules.

Fertilize

Fertilize X means putting X Fertilize tokens, one at a time, on any legal blank Nodes you choose. Fertilize without a number means one token. You may choose the same blank Node twice; the second token creates Terrain immediately, so any remaining tokens must go elsewhere. A card may narrow the choice to blank Nodes adjacent to a specified Node or Terrain; without that text, there is no extra adjacency restriction. A token is not Terrain, does not count for adjacency, and cannot hold an Entity. At the beginning of a Verdant Patron's turn, before drawing, remove every Fertilize token still on a blank Node and create Verdant Terrain there from the active player's supply. The marked position remains valid even if its neighboring Terrain was removed. After all tokens resolve, resolve reactions and check both Patron goals. If Terrain is placed there first, remove the token without effect. A second Fertilize token on the same blank Node removes both tokens and creates Verdant Terrain immediately from the acting player's supply.

Victory

Each Patron pursues its faction's vision of Aurelia's future, expressed as a goal in the shared Terrain layout. Check both goals after each fully resolved action. The first player to fulfill their Patron's goal wins immediately. If both goals become fulfilled after the same action, the players share victory.

See for faction visions, and for Entanglements and Overload.

Factions

Each faction offers a different way to shape the shared Terrain layout. Faction identity guides card design without restricting a deck's Entity and Event cards or its separate Terrain supply.

Verdant

Verdant

Civilization should support more life than it consumes. Verdant turns Terrain into a resource for growth and control.

Vision

Card identity

  • Terrain is a resource to spend, remove, or reshape for value.
  • A slow start grows stronger as more Terrain cards enter the shared layout.
  • Many cantrips offer a small effect and draw a card.
  • Fertilize marks a blank Node for delayed Verdant growth; a second token there creates Verdant Terrain immediately.
  • Verdant relies on relatively few Entities.
  • Control effects remove cards, turn off effects, and transform Terrain.
Radiance

Brightworks

The future must be consciously built and responsibly powered. Brightworks focuses on energy, infrastructure, efficiency, and managed strain.

Vision

Card identity

  • Lumens are a resource to spend or exchange through card effects.
  • Card effects combine into powerful sequences.
  • With relatively few Radiance Terrain cards, protection matters.
  • Some effects reward Radiance Nodes with no adjacent Radiance, creating separate energy islands.
  • Events are among the strongest Radiance cards.
  • Radiance favors fast, aggressive pressure.

These are card design goals; energy islands are an effect reward, not a placement rule.

Flow

Flowbound

No community can survive alone. Flowbound emphasizes water, movement, shared spaces, and chains of adjacent Nodes.

Vision

Card identity

  • Entities are a resource to spend, exchange, move, and repurpose.
  • Many Entities have When Placed effects and work together in chains.
  • Events are usually weaker than Entity effects.
  • Some card effects let Flow Entities occupy non-Flow Terrain.
  • Flow can take opposing Entities, use their effects, or turn them into Flow Entities.
  • Flow creates little new Terrain directly, but can transform existing Terrain into Flow.
  • Many Entities have When Shaped effects: archive one from hand to create its matching Terrain and resolve the effect.

These are card design goals.

Symbiosis

Dreamwardens

A future without memory repeats old catastrophes. Dreamwardens connect diverse Terrain, preserve and restore their network, and interact with the Archive.

Vision

Card identity

  • Entanglements connect Symbiosis Terrain to non-Symbiosis neighbors.
  • A slow start gives way to growing value from a connected network of Symbiosis Terrain cards.
  • Dreamwardens use relatively few Entities and many powerful Events.
  • Effects both give Nodes Crisis and remove Crisis.
  • Some cards create Terrain, but more transform existing Terrain into different types.

Card effects create Entanglements. See Glossary for valid pairs, limits, bridges and removal.

Looking for how the cards work during play?

Glossary

Card keywords, markers and connections in the shared world.

Horizon
Your face-down draw pile.
Archive
Your face-up pile for discarded cards, resolved Events, Dissolved Entities, and Entities used to Shape Terrain.
World
The Terrain cards on the table and the Entities placed on them, representing one region of Aurelia in the current match. Both Archives are outside the World. Blank positions are existing land not yet shaped.
Recall
Move an Entity or Event from its owner's Archive to the destination stated by an effect. Recall alone does not Evoke, Place, or Shape.
Fertilize
Fertilize X puts X tokens, one at a time, on any legal blank Nodes; Fertilize alone means one token. A card may require adjacency to a specified Node or Terrain. A second token on the same blank Node creates Verdant Terrain immediately; otherwise it grows at the start of a Verdant Patron's turn. Creating Terrain there first removes the token.
Create Terrain
Take a card from your separate Terrain supply and place it in a legal empty position. This differs from Transforming existing Terrain.
Evoke
Invoke an Entity or Event from hand. Resolve an Entity's When Evoked effect before Placing it; resolve an Event and then Archive it.
Place
Put an Evoked Entity on matching Terrain, then resolve its When Placed effect.
Shape
Archive an Entity from hand as a Play action to create its matching Terrain on a legal blank Node. Resolve only its When Shaped effect, if any.
Node
One Terrain card in the shared layout, including its Entity, Condition, Lumens, and markers.
Transform
Replace a shaped Node's Terrain, preserving its Entity and Condition unless an effect says otherwise. Remove its Lumens and Entanglements.
Board
The board has 4 rows and 5 columns: 20 spaces for Terrain cards. Terrain must stay within this boundary. A printed board is optional.
Adjacent
Terrain cards touching by a side or a corner are adjacent. The first Terrain may occupy any space on the 4 × 5 board; later Terrain must be adjacent to existing Terrain and remain inside the board.
Flow Entity
An Entity whose current Terrain identity is Flow. Check the Entity itself, not the Terrain underneath it. Its Patron, owner or controller does not affect whether it counts toward Flow’s vision.
Vision
A faction’s victory condition, shared by its Patrons. Resolve the full action, reactions and Overload before checking victory. Simultaneous fulfillment shares victory.
Lumens
A spendable resource attached to Radiance Terrain, not to its Entity. A Node normally holds up to 5. Only Lumens on Radiance Terrain without Crisis count toward the 15-Lumen vision. Destroying the Entity leaves Lumens in place. Destroying or Transforming its Terrain removes all Lumens, even when the replacement is also Radiance.
Overload
When a Radiance Node would hold more than 5 Lumens, Affect it with Crisis using the normal Crisis/Protection rules. Excess Lumens must immediately be spent through available legal effects or are lost; at most 5 remain afterward. Resolve this before checking victory.
Entanglement
A card effect links two adjacent Terrain cards: exactly one Symbiosis and one non-Symbiosis. Each Symbiosis Terrain can have only one Entanglement with one neighbor. A non-Symbiosis Terrain can link to several Symbiosis neighbors, but each pair can have only one Entanglement. Destroying or Transforming either Terrain removes the link. Changing or destroying only an Entity does not remove it.
Connected Symbiosis group
Symbiosis Nodes connect by side or corner adjacency. A non-Symbiosis Node with Entanglements to Symbiosis Nodes in different directions can extend the group by bridging those groups. Count each link once through its Symbiosis endpoint. The vision requires at least 5 Entanglements in one group and no Crisis on any entangled Symbiosis Node in that group. Non-Symbiosis bridges and unentangled Symbiosis connectors have no additional no-Crisis requirement.
